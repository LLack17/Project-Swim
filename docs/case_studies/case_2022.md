# Case Study: The 2022 Crash

Landon Lackner · Project Swim, Phase 2B · October 2026

## Summary

From December 2021 to September 2022, US stocks lost **29% after inflation** (25% before it) in nine months. It was the second inflation crash in our study, and it rhymed closely with 1973-74: easy money, a speculative boom, inflation the Fed called temporary, then the fastest rate rises in 40 years. Everything except cash and commodities lost money after inflation, and **bonds did worse than in 1973-74**: our 7-10 year Treasuries lost 20%, 20-year bonds 31%.

**For Project Swim**, 55/45 lost **25% after inflation** and took 3.6 years to recover, milder than 1973-74 (-40%, 12 years) but with the same weakness: the bonds fell with the stocks. **Gold failed this time** (-14% after inflation), which matters for the gold debate. Most of the 1973-74 patterns held; one did not (see Patterns check).

Numbers are from our case-study script (scripts/phase2b/case\_study.py, run 2026-10-16, all data checks passed).

## What happened

| Date | Event |
| --- | --- |

| Mar 27, 2020 | CARES Act, $2.2 trillion |
| Feb 4, 2021 | Larry Summers warns the relief plan could set off inflation |
| Mar 11, 2021 | American Rescue Plan, $1.9 trillion |
| Nov 30, 2021 | Powell drops the word "transitory" |
| Dec 2021 | US stocks peak, after inflation (our data, month-end) |
| Feb 24, 2022 | Russia invades Ukraine |
| Mar 2022 to Jul 2023 | Fed raises rates from about 0% to 5.25-5.5% |
| Jun 2022 | CPI inflation peaks at 9.1% |
| Sep 2022 | US stocks bottom, after inflation (our data, month-end) |
| Mar 10, 2023 | Silicon Valley Bank fails |

*The Fed started raising rates only after stocks had peaked.*

**The setup (2020-21).** When Covid hit in March 2020, the Fed cut rates to zero and bought trillions of dollars of bonds, and Congress passed about $5 trillion of relief, including $2.2 trillion in March 2020 and $1.9 trillion in March 2021. The money supply grew about 25% in a year, the fastest since World War II. With rates at zero and households flush, money poured into stocks, crypto, speculative growth companies and houses. By late 2021, inflation was near 7%, but the Fed still called it "transitory" until November.

**The fall (2022).** Stocks peaked at the end of 2021. In February 2022 Russia invaded Ukraine, and energy and food prices jumped. The Fed began raising rates in March, then moved in 0.75-point steps from June, the fastest increases since the early 1980s, while also shrinking its bond holdings. Inflation peaked at 9.1% in June. Stocks bottomed in September-October 2022.

**The aftershock.** The rate rises kept going into 2023 and broke banks that had loaded up on long-term bonds: Silicon Valley Bank failed in March 2023, five months after the stock bottom. The Fed's last increase came in July 2023, at 5.25-5.5%.

## Cause and effect, step by step

1. **A flood of money.** To fight the Covid shock, the Fed cut rates to zero and bought bonds, and the government sent households and businesses about $5 trillion. The money supply grew about 25% in a year. *Effect:* people and companies had far more money than there were goods to buy.
2. **Free money looks for a home.** With interest rates at zero, cash and bonds paid nothing. Money moved into stocks (especially speculative growth companies), crypto and houses. Our data at the peak: stock valuations (CAPE 38) in the top 1% of history, margin loans in the top 2%, real house prices rising at a record pace. *Effect:* fragility, in the same places as 1972.
3. **Inflation arrives, and is waved off.** Spending met supply chains that were still broken. Inflation reached 7% by the end of 2021, but the Fed called it "transitory" and kept rates at zero until March 2022. *Effect:* by the time the Fed moved, it had to move fast.
4. **The war adds a supply shock.** Russia's invasion of Ukraine in February 2022 pushed up oil, gas and food prices: the 2022 version of the 1973 oil embargo, though smaller.
5. **Rates jump, and anything long falls.** The Fed raised rates from near zero to 4.25-4.5% by December 2022, in the fastest increases since the early 1980s. The same arithmetic as 1973 applied: higher rates make future money worth less today. Bonds that started with yields near 1.5% had almost no income to cushion the price fall, so they fell harder than in 1973-74. Speculative growth stocks, worth only for profits far in the future, fell most.
6. **The bottom.** Inflation peaked at 9.1% in June 2022. Stocks bottomed in September-October, once markets could see inflation turning, even though the Fed was still raising rates. *Effect:* a shorter, shallower crash than 1973-74.
7. **The aftershock.** Banks that had bought long-term bonds with short-term deposits took the losses later: Silicon Valley Bank failed in March 2023. The same short-funding trap that broke the 1974 REITs and Franklin National.

## Before: warning signs at the peak, compared with 1972

Read at the December 2021 peak with publication lags; percentile = rank against that signal's own history up to then (100% = highest ever).

| Signal | Dec 2021 | Rank | Dec 1972 | Rank |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shiller CAPE | 38.3 | **99%** | 18.7 | 78% |
| Margin loans / GDP | 2.14% | **98%** | 1.04% | 96% |
| Private credit / GDP | 162% | 94% | 94% | **100%** |
| Real house prices, 12 months | +10.7% | **100%** | n/a |  |
| Profit margin (profits / GDP) | 12.4% | **100%** | 6.0% | 32% |
| CPI inflation | 6.9% | 89% | 3.4% | 70% |
| Core PCE inflation | 4.8% | 83% | 3.1% | 61% |
| Fed funds rate | 0.08% | 3% | 5.33% | 82% |
| Credit spread (Baa minus 10y) | 1.83 pts | 48% | 1.57 pts | 85% |
| Unemployment | 4.1% | 17% | 5.3% | 62% |
| Yield curve inverted in prior 2 years? | Briefly, Feb 2020 (the Covid shock, not this crash) |  | No |  |

**What this says.**

- **More warning lights were on in 2021 than in 1972.** Valuations, margin debt, house prices and profit margins were all at or near records at the same time.
- **Inflation was visible this time.** In 1972 it had cooled to 3%; in December 2021 it was 6.9% and rising. What was missing was a response: the Fed funds rate was 0.08% with inflation near 7%, an after-inflation rate of about -7%, far below 1972's +2%.
- **Record profit margins were a risk, not a comfort.** Margins that high usually fall back, which hits earnings just as rates rise.
- **Leverage again.** Margin debt was near a record in both peaks. Overall private credit was high (94th percentile) but not the standout it was in 1972; the borrowing this time was concentrated in governments and speculative pockets.
- **The yield curve again gave no useful warning.** Its only inversion came during the Covid panic in early 2020, two years earlier and for a different reason.

## During: who won and who lost, compared with 1973-74

| Asset | 2022 (Dec 2021 to Sep 2022) | 1973-74 (Dec 1972 to Sep 1974) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commodities (futures) | +5.3% | +131.5% |
| Cash (T-bills) | -4.7% | -4.7% |
| Treasuries 1y | -7.3% | -6.3% |
| Gold | -14.3% | +99.5% |
| **Treasuries 7-10y (ours)** | **-20.1%** | -14.9% |
| US stocks | -28.8% | -55.1% |
| Treasuries 20y | -30.9% | -27.2% |
| REITs | -31.7% | -42.6% |

*Return after inflation, stock peak to bottom. Source: our case-study script, deflated by CPI.*

**Depth and speed.** Stocks fell 25% in price terms and 29% after inflation over nine months, with prices up 5.5% along the way. That is about half the 1973-74 loss, in less than half the time.

**Bonds were worse than in 1973-74.** Our 7-10 year Treasuries lost 20% after inflation (15% in 1973-74), and 20-year bonds lost 31%, more than stocks. The reason is where yields started: about 1.5% in 2021 against 6.4% in 1972. A bond yielding 6% earns back a price fall quickly; one yielding 1.5% has almost no cushion. Shorter bonds again did best among Treasuries.

**Real assets mostly failed this time.** Commodities rose only 5% after inflation (they spiked after the invasion, then fell back by September), and gold lost 14%. In the 1970s gold started from a price fixed by law and rose as real interest rates went deeply negative; in 2022 real interest rates rose sharply, and gold, which pays no interest, lost its appeal. The only reliable protection was the shortest cash.

**Industries (Ken French 49, after inflation).** Best: coal (+71%), oil (+31%), agriculture (+12%), defence (-1%), fabricated products (-3%). Worst: toys (-56%), entertainment (-51%), clothing (-48%), textiles (-46%), real estate (-42%). As in 1973-74, the companies that dig or grow things did best and consumer and real-estate companies did worst.

## Policy response: late, then fast

|  | Fed funds rate | Federal budget (% of GDP) | Fed balance sheet |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A year before the peak (Dec 2020) | 0.09% | -14.5 (fiscal 2020) |  |
| At the peak (Dec 2021) | 0.08% |  | $8.76 trillion |
| At the bottom (Sep 2022) | 2.56% | -5.3 (fiscal 2022) |  |
| A year after the bottom (Sep 2023) | 5.33% | -6.1 (fiscal 2023) | $8.00 trillion |

**The Fed was late, then fast.** It held rates near zero for about ten months after inflation passed 5%, then raised them by more than five points in 16 months. Unlike 1973-74, the Fed was still raising rates at the bottom and for ten months after it.

**The government had already spent.** The deficit hit 14.5% of GDP in fiscal 2020, the largest since World War II, and was still 5-6% during the crash. In 1973-74 the deficit was tiny. That spending is part of why inflation got going, and part of why there was no official recession in 2022 despite the crash.

**The Fed began shrinking its balance sheet** in June 2022 (from $8.8 trillion to $8.0 trillion a year after the bottom), pulling back the money it had added.

**Same rule as 1973-74:** with inflation high, the Fed did not rescue markets; it fought prices. The one rescue came in March 2023, for depositors at Silicon Valley Bank and other failing banks.

## Our portfolio and the recovery

|  | 2022 | 1973-74 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 55/45 loss at the stock bottom, after inflation (monthly rebalancing) | **-24.8%** | -39.6% |
| Yearly / never rebalanced | -24.9% / -24.9% | -38.5% / -37.0% |
| 55/45 back to even, after inflation | Jul 2025 (**3.6 years**) | Dec 1984 (12.0 years) |
| US stocks back to the old peak, after inflation | Mar 2024 (2.2 years) | Mar 1983 (10.2 years) |
| US stocks back to the old peak, before inflation | Dec 2023 (2.0 years) | Dec 1976 (4.0 years) |
| After the bottom, per year after inflation: stocks / 55/45 (1 year) | +16.2% / +6.2% | +29.4% / +14.2% |
| Same, 3 years | +20.9% / +11.5% | +14.8% / +9.3% |

**The bonds cut almost nothing.** Stocks lost 29% and 55/45 lost 25%: holding 45% in bonds saved only four points, because the bonds themselves lost 20%. In 1973-74 the bonds at least saved 15 points. This is the clearest evidence yet that 55/45 has no protection in an inflation crash.

**55/45 took longer to recover than stocks.** Stocks were back in 2.2 years, but the portfolio needed 3.6, because the bonds kept losing as the Fed kept raising rates into 2023.

**Rebalancing made no difference** (all three within 0.1 point), consistent with 1973-74.

## Companies: who collapsed, who held up

**Collapsed or nearly did (2022 returns, S&P 500 members, to 22 December 2022):**

| Company | 2022 | What did it in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generac (generators) | -75% | **Pandemic boom reversed**: demand pulled forward, then sales fell |
| Match Group (dating apps) | -70% | **Price**: a high valuation on slowing growth |
| SVB Financial (bank) | -68%, then failed in March 2023 | **Borrowed short to hold long**: invested deposits in long-term bonds, which lost value as rates rose; depositors ran |
| Tesla | -64% | **Price**: the most valuable carmaker in the world at a valuation that assumed decades of growth |

**Held up:**

| Company | 2022 | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Occidental Petroleum | +113% | **Pricing power in inflation**: sells oil at whatever oil costs |
| Constellation Energy (power) | +108% | Electricity prices rose with gas prices |
| Arch Capital (insurer) | +41% | Insurers raised premiums and earned more on their bond holdings as rates rose |

*Returns are from a published list of 2022's best and worst S&P 500 stocks; the "what did it in" and "why" explanations are our reading, except SVB's (widely documented), and should be checked against company reports before use in an interview.*

**Checking the five company rules from 1973-74:**

1. **Price relative to growth: held.** The biggest losers were companies priced for perfection (Tesla, Match), like Polaroid and Avon in 1973.
2. **Pricing power in inflation: held.** Energy producers were again the big winners.
3. **How the company funds itself: held, with a delay.** SVB is a near-copy of the 1974 construction REITs and Franklin National: short-term money funding long-term assets. It broke five months after the stock bottom, as rates kept rising.
4. **Cash flow, not just earnings:** many 2021 favourites (recent listings, "SPACs") had no profits at all and fell sharply; we have not checked specific cash-flow cases yet.
5. **Honest accounts:** FTX, the crypto exchange, collapsed in November 2022 amid fraud charges, the Equity Funding of this cycle, though outside the stock market.

**Insurers this time:** Arch Capital's gain suggests insurers did well in 2022, the opposite of 1974-76 (GEICO). One difference: in 2022 insurers could raise prices quickly and earned more on their bonds as rates rose. The industry-wide number is still to check.

## What experts said at the time

| Who, when | What they said | How it turned out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Larry Summers (former Treasury Secretary), February 2021 | The $1.9 trillion relief plan could "set off inflationary pressures of a kind we have not seen in a generation" | **Right.** Inflation reached 9.1% in June 2022, the highest since 1981 |
| White House economic adviser Jared Bernstein, 2021 | Called Summers's inflation alarms "flat-out wrong" | **Wrong** |
| The Fed, through most of 2021 | Inflation was "transitory", caused by temporary supply problems; the word was dropped on 30 November 2021 | **Wrong on timing.** Inflation lasted well into 2023 |
| The Fed's own forecast, December 2021 | 2022 inflation of 2.6% (2.7% core) and a policy rate of 0.9% by the end of 2022 | **Wrong by a wide margin.** Inflation was roughly twice that and the policy rate ended 2022 at 4.25-4.5% |

**The pattern, again.** As in 1972-73, the officials were confidently wrong and a minority, arguing from money and stimulus, was right. The difference from 1973: the warnings this time were public, specific and made by a prominent mainstream economist, a year before the peak. The risk was not hidden; it was argued away.

## Patterns check: do the 1973-74 patterns hold in 2022?

| # | Pattern from 1973-74 | 2022 | Score after 2 cases |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Crash = fragility × trigger | Fragility: record valuations, margin debt, house prices. Triggers: inflation, the Fed's turn, the Ukraine war | **2 of 2** |
| 2 | Easy money → credit boom → overpriced favourites | Zero rates and money +25% → speculative growth, crypto, houses | **2 of 2** (in 2022 the borrowing was more by government than private) |
| 3 | With high inflation, the Fed tightens into the fall | Rates 0.08% → 2.56% during the fall, 5.33% a year later | **2 of 2** |
| 4 | "Duration" decides who loses most | 20y bonds -31%, speculative growth worst, energy best | **2 of 2**, stronger in 2022 because starting yields were so low |
| 5 | Borrowing short to hold long breaks when rates jump | SVB, March 2023 | **2 of 2**, but the break came after the stock bottom |
| 6 | Inflation crash: real assets protect, Treasuries don't | Treasuries failed; commodities +5%; **gold -14%** | **Half right.** Treasuries failed both times; real assets protected only in 1973-74 |
| 7 | The bottom comes when the policy squeeze peaks | Stocks bottomed while the Fed was still raising; it was **inflation** that had peaked (June 2022) | **Revised.** In 1974 the bottom came as the policy rate turned (inflation peaked later); in 2022 as inflation turned (rates kept rising). Better: the bottom comes when one of the two squeezes visibly turns |
| 8 | Inflation hides losses | 4.0 points of difference (-25% vs -29%) | Holds, but small when the crash is short |
| new | Inflation crashes are slow | 9 months in 2022, 21 in 1973-74; inflation was visible months before the worst | **2 of 2**; compare with the growth crashes (2000-02, 2007-09) next |

**The two most important updates:**

- **Gold is not a reliable inflation-crash hedge.** It worked when real interest rates were falling deep below zero (1973-74) and failed when they were rising fast (2022). What gold protects against is falling real rates, not inflation itself. This matters for test 2B-H's gold mixes.
- **Starting yields decide how much bonds can lose.** At 6% yields (1972), bonds lost 15% after inflation; at 1.5% yields (2021), they lost 20%. Today's 10-year yield is the number to watch for how exposed 55/45 is now.

## Lessons

**For an investor today**

1. **Inflation crashes rhyme.** Fifty years apart, the same chain: easy money, a boom in favourite assets, inflation officials call temporary, then a Fed that has to fight it. Recognising the chain early matters more than predicting the trigger.
2. **Bonds are only as safe as their starting yield.** At 1.5% yields, Treasuries had no cushion and lost more than stocks at the long end. In an inflation shock, bonds are a source of risk, not protection.
3. **Gold hedges falling real interest rates, not inflation itself.** It doubled in 1973-74 and lost 14% in 2022.
4. **The same company traits win and lose.** Energy producers won both times; companies priced for perfection, and lenders that funded long assets with short money, lost both times.
5. **Public warnings get argued away.** In 2021 the risk was stated plainly by a mainstream economist a year in advance and dismissed. Taking such warnings seriously, even when officials disagree, is an edge.

**For Project Swim (decisions stay with the pre-registered tests)**

- **55/45's weakness is confirmed twice.** In both inflation crashes the bonds fell with stocks; in 2022 they saved only four points. Test 2B-A (inflation protection) is now the most important test in the phase.
- **Test 2B-H's gold mixes face a real headwind.** Gold failed in 2022. The rule to require a gold mix to work in both halves of the sample (1970-1997 and 1998-2026) looks even more important now.
- **Test 2B-F (bond maturity):** short bonds beat long bonds in both inflation crashes. The growth crashes (2000-02, 2007-09) will show the other side.
- **Phase 3 screen:** the five company rules held up in a second crash; add a check for how much a company's value depends on distant profits (very high price-to-sales or no profits).
- **Watch today's starting yields** as a measure of how much protection the bond half can give.
