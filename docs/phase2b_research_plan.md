# Phase 2B research plan: crises, warning signs and what protects a portfolio

Written 2026-10-09, before any Phase 2B work began, and committed to GitHub before the first case study or test. Any change to this plan is added to the amendment log at the bottom with a date and a reason; nothing is removed.

## Why this phase exists

Phase 2 settled the allocation (55% US stocks / 45% intermediate Treasuries) and found its known gap: no protection in an inflation crash. This phase studies the major crises in depth to learn three things:

1. **Portfolio decisions:** should anything in the portfolio change?
2. **Warning signs:** which signals were visible beforehand, and which only look obvious in hindsight?
3. **Investor judgment:** how leverage, bubbles and policy shaped each crisis, and how long recovery took.

The AI boom is not covered here; it gets its own phase, which will also build the bubble screen (Greenwood, Shleifer and You, 2019).

## Part 1: Case studies (learn; cannot change the portfolio)

Six crises, each measured on the same template so that similarities and differences show side by side.

| Crisis | Type | Data |
|---|---|---|
| 1973-74, US | Inflation crash | FRED, Ken French, Shiller, JST |
| 2000-02, US | Valuation bubble, growth crash | FRED, Ken French, Shiller |
| 2007-09, US | Credit and housing crash | FRED, Ken French, Shiller |
| 2022, US | Inflation crash | FRED, Ken French, ETFs |
| Japan 1990 | Asset bubble, decades-long recovery | JST (annual) |
| Asia 1997 | Currency and foreign-debt crisis | Lighter written case; 1990s index and currency data where available |

**The template (applied to every full case):**

- **Before (6-24 months ahead):** stock valuations (Shiller CAPE in the US; dividend yield abroad); credit boom (private debt growth vs the economy) and credit spreads; the yield curve and the central bank's rate path; inflation; house prices; margin debt; share issuance; foreign debt and the currency; unemployment; corporate profit margins.
- **During:** an asset scorecard from peak to bottom (stocks, Treasuries by maturity, cash, gold, commodities, real estate); the policy response (central bank and government, and how fast); depth and speed of the fall; the 55/45 portfolio month by month, including a quick check of how rebalancing (monthly, yearly, never) changed the result.
- **After:** time to recover the previous peak, before and after inflation; returns from the bottom over 1, 3 and 5 years.
- **Companies:** 2-3 companies that collapsed and 2-3 that held up (for example, Lehman vs JPMorgan in 2008), and what separated them: debt, cash flow, earnings quality, valuation. These lessons feed Phase 3 stock selection.
- **What experts said at the time:** what the central bank, major forecasters and well-known investors said before and during the crisis, and why they were right or wrong. This is the check against hindsight: a warning sign only counts if it was visible and argued at the time.

Each case ends with a one-page write-up: what happened, which warning signs were real, what experts got wrong, which companies survived and why, what protected portfolios, what policymakers did, and the lesson for an investor today. A final synthesis compares all six.

## Part 2: Decision tests (can change the portfolio)

Each test has a benchmark rule written now. Results that meet the benchmark are acted on. Landon may decide against a benchmark only by writing a **judgment exception** (the decision and the reason) before acting, as with gold in Phase 2. Every test records predictions first, and its script is committed before it runs. Tests are labeled 2B-A to 2B-G (2B-C, rebalancing, was folded into the case-study replays before the plan was committed; see the template).

| Test | Question | Data | Benchmark rule | If met |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2B-A Inflation-crash protection** | Do managed futures, REITs or commodities protect in inflation crashes without costing too much otherwise? | AQR time-series momentum factor (managed-futures proxy), FTSE Nareit REIT index, commodity indexes. **Data check first:** if the free trend-following data starts in 1985, it covers only one inflation crash (2022); look for longer data, and if none is available, state in the results that the test rests on one episode | The three Phase 2 criteria used for gold (Test 19): positive and better than cash in most high-inflation stock selloffs; a 10% position cuts the worst drawdown at a cost of at most 0.5 points a year; the improvement holds in both halves of the sample | Add up to 10% (fund rule of 2026-10-08) |
| **2B-B Warning signs** | Do the "before" signals predict crashes, or only look obvious afterwards? | JST, 16 countries, 1950-2020; signals fixed in advance, built on early decades and checked on later ones | A signal clearly raises the odds of a 30%+ real stock decline within 3 years, out of sample | Added to the monthly watch list (watch-only) |
| **2B-D Geography** | Does owning stocks outside the home market reduce Japan-style disaster risk? | JST, 16 countries, 1950-2020 (home-only vs global stock holdings); Ken French developed-markets data, 1990-2026, for a US investor | Global holdings shorten the longest time underwater for home investors in most of the 16 countries, and cost a US investor at most 0.5 points a year since 1990 | Consider a non-US stock fund, up to a third of the stock sleeve |
| **2B-F Bond maturity** | Which Treasuries protected best in each kind of crash: 1-3 years, 7-10 years (current), or 20+? | FRED yields (month-end), 1977-2026; SHY, IEF and TLT returns from 2002 | An alternative cuts the worst drawdown by 1+ point across both growth and inflation crashes, at a cost of at most 0.3 points a year | Change the bond fund |
| **2B-G Stock style** | Which kinds of stocks held up in crashes: value, quality, low-volatility or growth? | Ken French portfolios, US 1963-2026 and international 1990-2026 | A style beat the market in at least 3 of the 4 US crashes and did not lag badly over the full period | Phase 3 screening criteria |

## Rules for this phase

- **Order:** case studies first (1973-74 and 2022, then 2000-02, 2007-09, Japan 1990, Asia 1997), then the decision tests. Phase 3 stock selection runs in parallel; 2B-G feeds it as soon as it is done.
- **Rule freeze:** the Phase 2 watch-only rules stay frozen until 2027-10-08. Portfolio changes in this phase happen only through the tests above.
- **Midpoint review:** after the 2007-09 case (the four US cases done), compare hours spent with the plan. If behind, Japan 1990 and Asia 1997 become short written cases so Phase 3 is not starved.
- **Limits:** history has few independent crises, so every result is reported with that caveat. Annual cross-country data understates drawdowns.
